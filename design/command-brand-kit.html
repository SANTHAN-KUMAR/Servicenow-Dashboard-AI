02

Report inventory, measured live

Before designing the chart engine we queried sys_report on the live instance: 2,368 reports across 24 chart types. The question we set out to answer was which chart type belongs to which kind of report, so that generation could follow the existing convention.

There is no convention. That finding is what the rest of this section is about, and it decides the architecture.

The conclusion this forces. There is no field-to-form mapping stored anywhere on the instance, because the product has no such logic. sys_report.type records an author's choice, not a property of the data. So "read the existing mapping and follow it" is not merely blocked by access, it is impossible in principle. A selector driven by the measured shape of the data is not one option among several. It is the only approach with a defensible input, and it is what section 03 specifies.
03

The form engine

Given a table and a field, decide the chart without a human in the loop. Four inputs, all of which either already exist in the current build or are a single dictionary lookup away. No tagging, no configuration table, no per-report authoring decision.

Input 01

Field type

sys_dictionary.internal_type. Boolean, choice, reference, glide_date_time, integer, decimal, string. One lookup, cached per table.

Needs building, trivial

Input 02

Measured shape

Distinct count, top share, concentration. Computed over the real rows, not over the schema, so a 40-value choice field with 3 values in use reads as 3.

Already built, profileField()

Input 03

Aggregate

COUNT, SUM, AVG or COUNT DISTINCT. Determines whether parts sum to a meaningful whole, which is what makes part-to-whole legal or illegal.

Already available

Input 04

Row count n

The ACL-checked row count behind the aggregate. Drives the low-n caveat and suppresses forms that need more data than exists.

Already built

Guards that run after selection

The form is chosen, then the data is checked against it. These are the rules that stop a technically valid chart from being a dishonest one, and they are the reason the output can be trusted unattended.

n < 30Attach a low-n caveat chip. The current build shows a 440-day mean from 46 of 13,986 records with no warning at all.
Null or empty groupMerge to a single Unknown bucket, paint it the reserved neutral, pin it last regardless of size.
Part-to-whole with a non-additive aggregateRefuse. AVG segments do not sum to a whole, so the form is illegal here.
Top share > 0.9Demote from part-to-whole. A pie that says "one slice is nearly all of it" wastes a circle.
Series count > 6Fold the tail to Other or facet. A seventh hue is never generated.
Scatter, bubble or choroplethCap at 3 series. Measured: only the first three slots clear the all-pairs colour gate.
Partial trailing periodMark it on the chart. An in-progress month always looks like a collapse.
Aggregate against ACL countCompare and flag any divergence. GlideAggregate does not enforce row-level ACLs.
How to prove it before shipping. Run the rule table over all 2,368 live reports and produce a diff of chosen form against authored form. Two useful outputs fall out. Where the engine agrees with the author, that is a regression baseline. Where it disagrees, that is a reviewable list of the instance's worst existing charts, starting with the 20 datetime fields currently rendered as a single score. That diff is a deliverable in its own right, and it costs one script.

Thumbnails are generated, never screenshots

Each card renders a miniature from the dashboard's own chart spec, through the same form engine. It cannot go stale, costs nothing to store, and it is the difference between a catalog and a list of links.

Entitlement is membership, not a padlock

In-cell data bars sit behind the number

The bar gives you the shape of the column at a glance, the number stays exactly readable. Power BI offers a bars-only mode; we do not, because it throws away precision for nothing.

Colour scale, not a traffic light

Mean age uses the sequential ramp, so brightness is magnitude. Status red and green are reserved and never leak into a measure column, which is what stops a report looking like a spreadsheet someone conditionally formatted by hand.

Variance carries direction, not just sign

Backlog going up is bad, so +19.4% is red and -11.4% is green. The polarity is a property of the measure and is declared once, not decided per report.

Subtotals and a grand total are structural

Every group closes with a subtotal and the matrix closes with a total. This is what makes a report reconcilable, and it is the first thing a finance or audit reader checks.

Hierarchy is expandable, and shown as such

Open groups carry a down caret and indented children, closed groups a right caret. Nothing about the depth of the data is hidden from the reader.

Unassigned is never a group

2,600 records with no owner sit in the reserved neutral, with no data bar and no SLA figure, because there is no group to attribute them to. Averaging them into a real group would flatter every number above.

07

Material

What makes a dark interface look built rather than merely dark. Four ingredients, applied without exception. This is the part a flat card grid never has, and the part people read as expensive without being able to name.

01 Light source

Lit from above

Every panel carries a top-to-bottom surface gradient and a one pixel inset highlight on its top edge. One consistent light direction across the whole product is what makes surfaces read as physical rather than as coloured rectangles.

02 Edge

Gradient hairline

Borders fade from 18% white at the top edge to under 2% at the bottom, matching the light. A uniform one pixel grey border is the single most common tell of a flat template.

03 Grain

Film, not vector

A fractal noise overlay in overlay blend across the whole document. It removes the plastic flatness of pure CSS gradients and gives large dark fields something to hold. Zero network cost, since it is an inline SVG filter.

04 Restraint

Colour is scarce

The entire interface is graphite and ink. Buttons, tabs, chips, borders and axes carry no hue at all, so the only saturated pixels on screen belong to data. Nothing glows. Luminosity is what makes a dark dashboard read as a prototype rather than as a product.

Surface stack

--void page ground
--ground app canvas
--surface panel, lit gradient
--surface-3 inset, track, code

Nothing is pure black. #000 has no room beneath it, so shadow stops working and the whole stack collapses flat.

Elevation and radius

e1 restinginset top highlight, 1px black, 16px ambient
e2 hoverlift 3px, edge brightens, ambient deepens
e3 heroadds a faint signal ring. Exactly one per view.
Radius4 chip, 8 control, 14 panel, 16 window. Nothing rounder.
Nevera shadow on text, a blur on chrome, or a second light source
08

Colour

Four colour jobs, four separate palettes, no overlap. Every value was run through an OKLab and CVD validator against the surface it actually renders on, #0D1117 for dark and #FFFFFF for light. The light theme is a selected counterpart with its own steps rather than an inversion of the dark ones, and it clears the same gates independently. The toggle in the header switches the whole document.

Categorical, six slots plus a reserved neutral

Assigned in fixed order and never cycled. The ordering is the colour-blindness mechanism, not taste: slots alternate between the families that protanopia and deuteranopia collapse toward, so adjacent series stay separable. A seventh category is never a generated hue, it folds into Other.

Two steps per hue, and they are not interchangeable. The base step is the only one allowed to fill an area: bars, bands, ribbons, stacked segments. The second step is for strokes and markers, and it exists for a narrow technical reason rather than for effect: a two pixel mark does not hold the same perceived weight as a large fill of the same colour, so it needs a step of separation. The direction of that step flips by theme. On dark it goes lighter, on light it goes darker. Filling a large area with the dark theme's lighter step is what makes a dark dashboard physically tiring within a minute.

Colour follows the entity, never the rank

Network is Cyan on every chart on every dashboard. Filtering a series out must not repaint the survivors, because that is how a reader loses their place mid-analysis.

One saturated hue per panel by default

A ranked chart of one measure is one hue. Colour enters only when the categorical dimension is itself the encoding: a breakdown, a stack, a flow, a multi-series line.

Text never wears the data colour

Values, axis labels and legends stay in ink tokens. Identity comes from a coloured mark beside the text, never from tinting the text itself.

Never a dual axis

Two measures at different scales become two charts, small multiples, or an indexed common base. It is the single most misleading construction in enterprise BI, which is why the Pareto in section 02 is drawn without one.

Chrome carries no hue

Buttons, tabs, chips, borders and axes are graphite or ink. A coloured control competes with the data for the one thing colour is reserved to mean here.

Nothing emits light

No glow, no bloom, no coloured drop shadow, on chrome or on data. It reads as a demo rather than as a system of record, and it costs legibility for nothing.

Accessibility is a workstream, not an inheritance. A canvas or SVG chart gets no ARIA, no keyboard traversal and no screen-reader support for free, and ServiceNow publishes no accessibility conformance report covering custom UI Builder pages or custom UXF components as a category, so that responsibility sits with whoever builds them. The table-view toggle, focus order and per-mark labelling above are the mitigation and they get built alongside the charts. A dark-first system also needs its light counterpart specified before UAT rather than discovered in it.
11

Stack and delivery

With the client's agreement to permit external third-party components, the ceiling that was capping visual quality is gone. Two things the old constraint bundled together are worth separating, because only one of them carries real risk.

Loading assets from outside, now unblocked

Typefaces, charting libraries, WebGL renderers, animation engines. This is code and fonts travelling into the browser, with no instance data involved. It is what the agreement buys us, and essentially all of the visual quality gain lives here.

Sending data outside, still worth refusing

Record data leaving the instance for an external rendering or analytics service. Different risk class entirely: residency, DPA, ACL enforcement at the boundary. It also buys almost no visual quality. Recommendation is to keep aggregation and rendering in-browser, so instance data never leaves even though the code arrives from a CDN.

Render budget

A dashboard is left open all day on machines the client does not control, often several tabs at once, sometimes on a wall display that never sleeps. Visual effects that cost a repaint per frame are therefore a functional defect, not a taste question. These four rules were added after an earlier draft of this very document pinned a workstation.

RuleWhy
No mix-blend-mode A blend layer forces the compositor to read back everything beneath it and recomposite on every paint. On a long page it never stops costing. Any texture effect is achieved with a flat low-opacity layer instead.
No backdrop-filter A blurred backdrop on a sticky or fixed element is re-rendered every scroll frame. Use a near-opaque background; at these surface values it is visually indistinguishable.
No coloured drop-shadow on marks An SVG filter on every data mark multiplies rasterisation work by the number of marks, which is exactly the direction that scales badly. It was also the main reason an earlier draft read as a prototype.
content-visibility: auto per panel Off-screen panels are not laid out or painted at all. On a long report this is the single largest win available and it changes nothing visible.
No infinite animation A looping pulse or shimmer keeps the compositor awake forever, which on a wall display means it never idles. Every animation here runs once and stops.
Canvas above roughly 1,000 marks SVG gives crisp text and per-mark DOM events, and stops scaling around a thousand nodes. ECharts switches renderer on the same threshold, so the decision is a config flag rather than a rewrite.
Governance. The client's UI standards document permits custom UI where a business requirement cannot be met by standard components. Record the decision in exactly those terms. OOB Now Design System components cannot meet the stated visual and interactivity bar: the Data Visualization palette has fixed treatment, no free-form composition, and no sankey, stream, bump, radar or waterfall grammar. Custom components are therefore used per the governance document's own exception clause, with no OOB component modified and every deviation recorded with its justification. Argue from kind, not count. The OOB palette is roughly 16 types in Visualization Designer and 29 in classic reporting, which is not small, and the live instance uses 24 of them. Arguing from a low chart count is both wrong and easy to refute. The defensible claim is the one section 02 measures: the palette is wide, five types carry 77.4% of real reporting, and four of the forms this system needs have zero instances because the product cannot draw them.
What this does not claim. Not Power BI parity. What is achievable is Power-BI-grade visual and interaction quality on ServiceNow-native data. Cross-source semantic modelling, DAX-equivalent calculated measures and genuine end-user ad-hoc exploration are structural gaps that better rendering does not close. State them plainly now rather than discover them in UAT.
